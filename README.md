### Introduction
Agriculture underpins the economic stability of nations worldwide, and the sustained growth of the global population has intensified the need to increase food production. Global population is projected to approach 9.7 billion by 2050, requiring agriculture to produce 50% more food, feed, and fibre than was produced in 2012 (FAO, 2025). Consequently, understanding how factors such as climatic conditions, rainfall, pesticide usage, and soil properties (including type and pH) influence crop yield is of critical importance. Rainfall, soil nutrient content (e.g. potassium, phosphorus, nitrogen), soil pH, precipitation, and temperature have been consistently identified as key determinants of crop growth, yield, and productivity across the literature (Nature Scientific Reports, 2025). Identifying the trends and relationships underlying these variables can inform predictive modelling and support evidence-based decision-making in efforts to address global food insecurity.

### Aims and Objectives
The aims of this study were to:
- Conduct an exploratory data analysis (EDA) to identify patterns and relationships among the variables influencing crop yield over time.
- Develop an interactive dashboard to visualise the relationship between crop yield and pesticide usage across continents, countries, and crop types over time.
- Construct a machine learning model to predict crop yield (hg/ha) as a function of key independent variables, namely crop type, average rainfall (mm/year), average temperature (°C), and pesticide usage (tonnes).

### Data Source
The dataset used in this study was sourced from Kaggle, comprising publicly available data originally compiled by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the World Bank.
